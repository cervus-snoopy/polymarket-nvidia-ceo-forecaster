Design decisions taken at 00:27 WIB (recorded here so the transcript audit
can follow them):
  D1. Python 3.13, stdlib only (json/math/os/sys). No numpy — nothing is
      installed before the program runs.
  D2. Beat the baseline on three axes, all estimated from the case's own
      history only (no network, no hardcoded market knowledge):
      (a) per-market, per-horizon volatility from empirical H-step change
          distributions (H in {1,5,30,120,240}), inner scale from IQR
          (jump-robust), outer scale from 5%-95% range (jump-aware);
          1-minute scale from MAD over the last 24 h; log-linear
          interpolation in horizon;
      (b) shrunk drift: mean 1-min change over last 6 h, winsorized,
          shrunk 0.7x toward 0, clipped to +/-0.0002/min;
      (c) cross-market sum discipline: 24 mutually exclusive medians should
          sum to ~1; shift 40% of the excess beyond 0.02 back toward 1.
  D3. Normal z per level; inner levels (0.25/0.5/0.75) use inner scale,
      outer levels (0.05/0.95) use outer scale.
  D4. Defensive output: clamp [0,1], round 6 dp, force non-decreasing,
      atomic write via .tmp + os.replace, size check vs 1,048,576 bytes.
  D5. Determinism: no randomness, no wall-clock, no dict-order dependence
      (markets emitted in input order).

00:30 WIB — FIX 1: tail-aware outer scale at horizon 1
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Change: outer[1] = max(MAD sigma, (q95-q05 of 1-min diffs)/(2*1.644854))
  instead of outer[1] = MAD sigma. Rationale: MAD sees only the typical
  tick; the empirical 5%-95% range of the same diffs captures the jump tail.
  Re-test: entry 1.356495 vs baseline 1.554134 (-12.72%), coverage 0.7595.
  Effect small (short horizons contribute little loss mass) but principled;
  kept.
